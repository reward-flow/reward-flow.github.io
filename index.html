01 THE IDEA

Better alignment.
Room for diversity.

A single prompt has many good answers.
Its reward should reflect that.

Abstract

Post-training alignment is important for flow-based text-to-image generation, but existing GRPO-style methods usually optimize scalar rewards and tend to concentrate the policy on a few reward-favored samples. This limits diversity, weakens generalization to unseen preference metrics, and becomes unstable under multi-reward optimization.

We introduce RewardFlow, a reward distribution matching framework that aligns the flow policy with a reward-induced distribution over sampled denoising trajectories. Multiple high-quality generations can receive positive optimization signals. A warm-started, prompt-conditioned partition function calibrates rewards, while a step-wise KL constraint preserves the pretrained flow prior.

Experiments on SD3.5-M and FLUX.1-dev, under both single- and multi-reward training, show stronger preference alignment, better generalization, and more diverse generations.

From rewards to distributions.

01 / DISTRIBUTE

Match reward distributions

Turn trajectory rewards into positive probability weights, so more than one high-quality sample can contribute to learning.

02 / CALIBRATE

Learn the normalization

03 / PRESERVE

Keep the flow prior

An external step-wise KL constraint anchors denoising transitions to the frozen pretrained model for stable optimization.

Experimental setup +

Both backbones are trained for 600 epochs on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs. The prompt batch size is 9 for SD3.5-M and 3 for FLUX.1-dev, with 24 trajectories per prompt. The reward coefficient is β = 1 and the step-wise KL weight is λKL = 0.1.

Human-preference experiments use official HPDv2 prompts: 107K training prompts and 3,200 evaluation prompts, evenly divided into Animation, Concept-art, Painting, and Photo. GenEval and OCR use their task-specific datasets. All compared methods use the same training and evaluation splits for each task.
